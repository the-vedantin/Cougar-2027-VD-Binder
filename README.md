# Cougar Racing — 2027 Vehicle Dynamics Binder

The design binder for the 2027 car's suspension and vehicle dynamics: one HTML page, generated
from the team's suspension model, that puts every computed number, every figure and the full
design record in one searchable place.

**Read it online: https://the-vedantin.github.io/Cougar-2027-VD-Binder/**

## What changed in this publication (v164, 2026-10-11)

- **v164 = the smallest combined change that cuts steering effort with the rack ratio fixed at 5.1 in/rev.** Two
  hardpoints move per side: the steering-arm end (tie-rod outer) goes to the longest arm the 12.7 mm joint-to-rim rule
  allows, and the upper ball joint moves 4.75 mm forward, trimming caster and mechanical trail. The rack stop opens
  to keep the hairpin lock. Camber, toe, tyre pressure, springs, bars and the rack itself are untouched. Hand torque at
  2 g with aero drops by a third; the steering chapter and the open items carry the before/after and the levers that
  were tried and rejected (a longer arm breaks the rim rule, less trail than this was ruled out, camber buys little).
- **Animated progression.** The design record (chapter 14) opens with `figs/progression_v4_to_v164.gif`: every saved
  version from the first 2027 file to v164, one frame each, rendered from the Vahan 3-D view with one camera.
- **Steering wheel size is now an input.** The steer block carries the wheel OD; every hand-torque figure also reports
  the force at the rim, and the hand-wheel angle to the stop comes from the saved rack stop and travel per turn.
- **Tyre file guard.** The app silently ran on the alphabetically first tyre file whenever a project was loaded at a
  pressure other than the panel default. Fixed, with a regression gate that checks the loaded tyre's identity. No
  published binder was affected (all were at the default pressure).

## What changed in the previous publication (v162, 2026-10-11)

- **Loads are corrected.** The tyre self-aligning torque (TTC Mz at each corner's solved slip and load) is now applied to
  the upright free body in the member solve, in the same sense as the side force acting behind the steer axis. Earlier
  binders solved the members without it and under-read the tie rod by a factor of three to four at the cornering limit;
  the arms move by about 15 %. The steering-effort chapter always carried it, and the member solve now agrees with it
  within 10 %.
- **Steering is on the real rack.** The rack travel per turn is 5.1 in/rev (129.5 mm/rev). Every hand-torque number in
  earlier binders assumed a 60 mm/rev placeholder and was about 2.2 times too low. The rack bar is 31.75 mm.
- **Ride rate uses a measured tyre rate.** The tyre vertical rate is 110 N/mm at 12 psi, fitted from the TTC loaded-radius
  channel of the chosen tyre on the 7 in rim at zero slip and camber (100 / 110 / 119 N/mm at 10 / 12 / 14 psi), replacing
  a 909 lbf/in placeholder. The road inputs of the Road & Ride sub-chapter remain ISO 8608 class-centre scenarios, not a
  measured track; the absolute tyre-load deviation there is a scenario number and the class-B rows are far rougher than
  any paved track.
- **Methods chapter.** Chapter 18 explains how each result is computed, not just which equation it rests on; chapters 03 and 05 gained the anti-geometry and ride equations.
- **Open item carried.** The rack housing as placed sits 10.4 mm inside the bulkhead keep-out solid while clearing the
  drawn front bay tubes by 5 mm; no rack height clears both (chapter 16).

## Viewing it locally

Download **both** `index.html` **and** the `figs/` directory and keep them side by side (clone the
repository, or download the ZIP from GitHub). Then open `index.html` in a browser. The page has no
external scripts, stylesheets or fetches, so it works offline from a local file; the only external
references are the relative `figs/…png` image paths, which is why `figs/` must sit next to it.
Every figure referenced by the page is present in `figs/` (checked at commit `0e34636`: 215 referenced, 215 present, none missing).

## How the page is organised

The left sidebar lists the chapters; the **search box** filters sections by text and shows "No
sections match" when nothing fits. The sidebar's **model hero** is the car captured from the
Vahan 3-D view: drag to rotate, scroll to zoom, and click a part to jump to its chapter (legend:
dashed = damper, grey = rocker, red = pushrod, red dash-dot = anti-roll bar, gold = tie rod).

## Interactive tools on the page

- **Load-case explorer (chapter 10).** Two sliders (lateral g in 0.1 steps, longitudinal g in
  0.2 steps, negative = braking), a wheel-bearing-spacing selector (40 / 50.8 / 60 / 70 / 80 mm)
  and "jump to" buttons for the standard cases (2.0 g cornering, 1.6 g braking, 1.0 g
  acceleration, 1.4 g + 1.0 g combined, static). Every slider position is a **pre-solved state**
  of the whole car through the same path as the Vahan Loads page (dynamics solve with the aero
  load at that state's speed, then every link, rocker bearing, damper, drop link, bar torsion,
  ball joint, wheel bearing and caliper bolt at all four corners); the page interpolates nothing.
  A dash means the solver could not balance that state. Positive lateral g is a left turn.
- **Model hero** (sidebar): rotate / zoom the captured car, click a part for its chapter.
- **Search** (sidebar): filters chapters and sections live.
- **Collapsible math blocks** throughout.

## Model and data provenance

- **Analysis settings (2026-10-10):** every grip, load, lap-time and aero number on the page uses a
  belt-to-asphalt road-grip derate of **0.75** (the earlier publication used 0.70) and the aero package
  **1,000 N at 45 mph** (72.4 km/h; earlier 88.5 km/h), 54 % on the rear axle. Only these two inputs
  changed: the hardpoints, springs and bars are the locked v151.5 file. The lap-sim set now also runs the
  MI 2018 endurance map (RoseLap DXF, 1,573 m) and the lap table carries the same package at the derate
  ±0.05 on every track.
- **"g with aero" convention (2026-10-10):** the G-G envelope and every "with the package" grip limit now use the
  same convention as the Vahan app: the package scales with speed on the Dynamics-panel turn radius (10 m), so the
  limit is the car on that corner. The earlier publication applied the full 1,000 N regardless of speed (2.24 g);
  on the app's convention it is 1.94 g.
- **Tyre comparison (chapter 17):** eleven tyre fits from the TTC data on the same v151.5 car. At the team's
  decision the tyres in that chapter are named by maker, size and compound (R20 / R25B / LC0 and the other
  candidates); only derived numbers are shown, never raw data, file names or run numbers. The rest of the
  binder keeps the de-identified single-tyre presentation. No wear is measured on the rig; the wear figures
  are proxies and say so. The tyre-level numbers in that chapter are measured load-bin peaks, not the gridded
  tyre model: the model clamps the force curve beyond an assumed peak-slip line and under-reads stiff compounds
  at light load (R20 at 224 N: 2.36 vs 2.62 measured). That is logged as an open item for the dynamics.
- **Geometry:** the saved Vahan project file v151.5 (the locked version). v151.5 is v151 with one change:
  the rear anti-roll-bar torsion tube sits in front of the rear rocker instead of behind it. The bar
  pivot was mirrored through the plane of the rear rocker plate; the arm end, the drop link and every
  other hardpoint are untouched, so the arm length, the bar motion ratio and the rear bar rate are the
  same numbers and no kinematic, dynamic or load result changed (the bar twists the other way).
  v151 is v150 with the rear upper ball joint and
  the rear pushrod upright end raised 4.8 mm (inboard pickups, front corners and every anti-roll-bar
  hardpoint untouched) and both anti-roll bars kept at 12.7 mm OD with the bore closed from 8.51 to
  6.79 mm (bar rates +15 %), giving a roll gradient of 0.60 °/g and 0.6° of camber per degree of
  roll on both axles. v150 was v149 plus four small, behaviour-held moves (front lower-arm inboard
  pickups out along the arm line, a 6.4° flip of the rear actuation plane about the pushrod outer
  end, a front upper-arm aft pickup slid 10 mm along its own axis, a 1.1 mm tie-rod tweak) and a
  working aero assumption. Current computed results are generated through Vahan; the page also contains stated assumptions, design targets and historical studies.
- **Tyre data:** FSAE Tire Test Consortium data measured at the Calspan Tire Research Facility.
  Per the TTC participant agreement the published figures are **de-identified**: the tyre make,
  model and compound are withheld. Please do not attempt to re-identify the tyre or repost the
  tyre-derived figures separately from this acknowledgment. Above the highest tested load the
  surfaces are extrapolated and the page says so where it matters.
- **Grip basis:** grip numbers are quoted asphalt-first with a 0.70 belt-to-asphalt multiplier
  applied to the measured belt data; where a chapter uses raw belt grip (steering, transient) it
  says so. The multiplier is a team assumption, not a measurement.
- **Aero:** 1,000 N at 88.5 km/h with 54 % on the rear axle is the team's working assumption of
  2026-09-29 (it was 350 N through v149); that downforce exists only at that speed.
- **CG height** (270 mm) and the driver mass are model inputs saved in the design file, not
  measurements of the finished car, and the page says to check them on the real car.
- **Engine model:** the torque curve is simulated (1-D engine solver), not dyno-validated; the
  lap-sim absolute times inherit that uncertainty.
- **Rigid model:** links, upright, chassis and steering are rigid in the model; there is no
  compliance, no tyre relaxation, and loads are quasi-static. There is no frame-stiffness input
  anywhere: the chassis-stiffness table (chapter 05) gives the roll stiffness the frame has to
  react and a ≥ 5× rule of thumb for its torsional stiffness, computed from that row. Earlier
  binders printed a typed 2,000 ft·lbf/deg as "the lap model's frame stiffness"; that figure was a
  RoseLap-era input and is not used by Vahan.

## Historical versus current analyses

- The **Design record** (chapter 14) keeps every saved revision from v72 to v151.5, grouped into
  eras, with what each changed and what it cost. Superseded revisions are kept as record and are
  labelled superseded; they are not the current design.
- A few cross-check figures are explicitly marked **"NOT RE-RUN FOR v151.5"** (for example the
  gearshift-cost figure from the v16-era car, kept only as a method cross-check). Treat any
  number so marked as historical, not as a v151.5 result.
- The **Open items** chapter is the authoritative list of what is still not passing on v151.5 as of
  the regression net and the review of the saved file; nothing there is hidden or waived.

## Open items on v151.5 (as published)

Summarised from chapter 16; read the chapter for the full statements and numbers.

- **Regression net on the saved v151.5** (2026-10-07): exit code 2, both failures the same front
  anti-roll-bar item below (the design-actuation gate and the Design-City self-gate report the
  same drop-link clash), plus 1 documented known-fail (26 archived v41–v69 configs float above
  the ground; superseded files, not edited).
- **Front ARB drop link vs rocker plate** near full bump: −1.7 mm at +50 mm wheel travel (the
  gate wants +3 mm), −2.4 mm at full damper bump, −3.3 mm with steering lock added. The designer
  suspects the gate itself (not yet investigated); no geometry was changed to hide it. The front
  bar's motion-ratio swing is treated as physical tuning via drop-link length, not a hardpoint
  change.
- **FSAE chassis bays** (frame members as clash bodies): nine negative pairs, all chassis
  routing problems rather than suspension-behaviour problems — the front rack bar into the
  lower rail at full droop (real rack-stub diameter needed), no front diagonal clearing the tie
  rod at bump + lock, rear diagonals through the driveshaft at droop, one rear diagonal into the
  diff/sprocket envelope. They need the chassis team to re-route members or the pickups to move.
- **Rod-end angles on the rear aft pickups:** over the 27° limit if the bearing bolt runs along
  the pivot axis (33.4° upper, 46.8° lower); 0° with the bolt normal to the arm plane, which is
  the decision taken.
- **Front rocker pivot bearing** carries 1.13–1.92× the damper force on the loaded front wheel
  (rear 0.42–0.89×).
- **Camber gain per g** reads 0.37 front / 0.35 rear deg per g against the 0.6 target. v151 was
  tuned to the other reading of that target, 0.6° of camber per degree of roll with roll at
  0.60 °/g, which it meets on both axles. If the target means −0.6° of kinematic camber at 1 g,
  it is not reachable inside the rim without moving inboard pickups (best −0.48 front / −0.43
  rear); the design record lists what was tried and rejected. Which definition applies is still
  the designer's call.
- **Grip targets:** on the asphalt basis the model gives 1.44 g per-corner / 1.58 g axle without
  aero (target 1.7 g) and 2.096 g axle with the 1,000 N package (target 2.1 g); on raw belt grip
  it meets both. The v151 geometry change did not move these numbers (the gap was shown not to be
  geometric). Whether the real car does depends on how much belt grip the track gives back,
  which the binder does not measure.
- **Reviewer questions answered in this build:** the "2,000 ft·lbf/deg" of earlier binders was a
  typed RoseLap-era input, not a model result (see *Rigid model* above); the "2,569 N" was the
  front pushrod of the v141 binder's 3 g bump row — the Loads chapter now names the v151.5 3 g bump
  pushrod / tie-rod / tyre loads per corner and quotes the v141 figure only as superseded.

## How the binder is produced and updated

The page and its figures are generated by a private script in the team's `DESIGN_2027/` folder
(not published) that drives the Vahan app headlessly on the design file and captures figures from the app. For this publication, embedded figures were extracted to external files under `figs/` so the published page stays small.
A publication replaces `index.html` and `figs/` together on `master`, which GitHub Pages serves
(this publication is commit `cb4d134`; the tyre-choice-less 2026-10-10 publication was `46e3904`, the earlier v151.5 publication at grip 0.70 was `04d5faa`, v151 `0a32306`, v150 `849a6fa`). This repository holds the published output only; it cannot
regenerate the binder on its own, and the design file, the generator and the tyre data are not
in it.
